# DevelopMonth Month 6 prototype testing plan

## Purpose

Test whether the static Month 6 concept feels useful, warm, understandable,
parent-controlled, and safe before building accounts or infrastructure.

This plan prepares testing; it does not claim that participant research has
already happened.

## Research questions

1. Can parents explain DevelopMonth in their own words after seeing This Month?
2. Does the product feel supportive rather than evaluative?
3. Can parents find Journal, Calendar, Check-in, Support Pack, Aion, and
   Summary?
4. Do parents understand that adding information is optional?
5. Does Aion feel parent-controlled rather than intrusive?
6. Is the boundary between education and professional care clear?
7. Do professionals see unsafe claims, omissions, or ambiguous signposting?
8. Which part would families return to each month?

## Month 6 validation focus

Month 6 is the proof month. The research goal is not to validate every month of
Year One at once. It is to determine whether one focused support pack can feel
useful, warm, parent-controlled, professionally bounded, and credible enough to
justify later infrastructure planning.

Do not move to account, database, analytics, payments, real Aion, or child-data
storage planning until the exit criteria in this document are met.

## Suggested participants

### Parent round

Start with 6–8 parents or primary carers:

- Mix of first-time and experienced parents
- Children across the first year, including around Month 6
- Different family structures and feeding experiences
- Different confidence with technology
- Include families with additional-needs experience where appropriate and
  supported

### Professional round

Start with 5–7 relevant professionals, for example:

- GP
- Public health nurse
- Paediatrician
- Speech and language therapist
- Occupational therapist or physiotherapist
- Paediatric dietitian or feeding specialist
- Child-development researcher

Professional participation must match the claims being reviewed. One reviewer
does not approve every domain.

## Session format

- 45 minutes
- Remote or in person
- Moderator and note-taker where possible
- Prototype on the participant’s own phone for at least part of the session
- No real child health information required
- Use the fictional Rory profile
- Ask permission before recording

## Parent session script

### Introduction

“We are testing the product, not you. This is a static concept: nothing you do
is saved. Please think aloud. Some links work, but forms and saving are
intentionally disabled.”

### Tasks

1. Start at **This Month**. Tell us what you think DevelopMonth is for.
2. Find one play idea you might try.
3. Find where you would keep a small family moment.
4. Find the next appointment.
5. Preview the monthly check-in. Explain how it makes you feel.
6. Open the full support pack and choose the most useful section.
7. Review the Aion example. Tell us what information it used.
8. Find how to stop or limit personalisation.
9. Open the monthly summary. Explain what it is—and what it is not.
10. Find the safety boundary and explain when you would contact a professional.

### Closing questions

- What felt most useful?
- What felt worrying, judgmental, or unclear?
- What would you remove?
- What would make you return next month?
- What would you never want DevelopMonth to store?
- Who else would you expect to be involved in reviewing this product?

## Professional review tasks

1. Review the Month 6 introduction for tone and accuracy.
2. Review each support-pack theme within professional scope.
3. Mark claims needing evidence, qualification, localisation, or removal.
4. Review the three-level signposting model.
5. Identify risks of false reassurance and unnecessary anxiety.
6. Review the Aion example for unsafe inference.
7. Review the monthly summary boundary.
8. List authoritative sources and expected review frequency.
9. Identify content outside the reviewer’s scope.
10. Add or verify relevant entries in the Month 6 source register.
11. Record whether the draft is suitable only for research or can progress
    toward parent-facing review.

## Observation scorecard

Score 1–5 and record evidence:

| Measure | Success signal |
| --- | --- |
| Product comprehension | Participant explains positive monthly support |
| Navigation | Completes 8/10 tasks without moderator rescue |
| Emotional safety | Describes experience as calm/supportive, not testing |
| Parent control | Understands what is optional and what information is used |
| Aion boundary | Does not interpret output as diagnosis or surveillance |
| Professional boundary | Knows when and how to seek professional guidance |
| Return value | Identifies at least one monthly reason to return |
| Mobile readability | Completes core tasks comfortably on a phone |

## Critical issues

Pause progression if:

- A participant believes the product diagnoses or assesses their child.
- A participant feels pressured to record everything.
- A participant cannot distinguish saved notes from published guidance.
- Signposting creates false reassurance or panic.
- A professional identifies unsafe feeding, sleep, urgent-care, or
  developmental guidance.
- Parent controls are not discoverable.

## Feedback record

For each issue capture:

- Participant type
- Prototype route
- Task
- Observation or short quote
- Severity: critical / high / medium / low
- Safety, usability, content, privacy, or accessibility category
- Proposed change
- Owner
- Decision and rationale
- Retest status

Do not place identifiable participant or child information in ordinary product
backlogs.

## Exit criteria for infrastructure planning

- No unresolved critical safety or privacy issue
- At least 80% task completion for core parent tasks
- Parent-control model understood by at least 80% of parent participants
- Professional reviewers approve content within their scope
- Month 6 pack has sources, named reviewers, version, and review date
- Key mobile and accessibility issues resolved and retested
- Clear evidence that the product creates repeat monthly value
