# Month 6 parent research session script

Status: draft script for research planning. Use reviewed consent/privacy wording
before recruiting participants or recording sessions.

## Session purpose

Test whether the static Month 6 DevelopMonth prototype feels useful, calm,
parent-controlled, understandable, and safe.

This session tests the product, not the parent and not the child.

## Session boundaries

Before starting, say:

> This is a static prototype using a fictional child profile called Rory.
> Nothing you type or click is saved or sent. Please do not share identifiable
> or sensitive information about a real child. DevelopMonth is educational
> support only and does not replace professional advice, diagnosis,
> developmental assessment, or treatment.

Confirm:

- participant is an adult;
- participation is voluntary;
- they can skip any question;
- they can stop at any time;
- recording choice is explicit;
- no real child health information is required.

## Setup

Record:

- participant code;
- device type;
- browser if known;
- session date;
- recording decision;
- prototype URL and deployed commit;
- moderator and note-taker.

Do not record names, child names, contact details, child health details, or
identifiable information in ordinary notes.

## Warm-up questions

1. When you look for parenting or child-development information, where do you
   usually go?
2. What makes that information feel trustworthy or untrustworthy?
3. What would make month-by-month support feel genuinely useful?

## Prototype tasks

Ask the participant to think aloud.

### Task 1: first impression

Start at `/prototype`.

Prompt:

- What do you think this product is for?
- Who do you think it is helping?
- What month or stage is this about?

Watch for:

- positive development understanding;
- confusion with medical assessment;
- pressure or milestone-test feeling.

### Task 2: find everyday support

Prompt:

- Find one play, movement, feeding, or bonding idea you might try.
- Tell me why it does or does not feel practical.

Watch for:

- usefulness;
- emotional tone;
- mobile readability.

### Task 3: journal concept

Prompt:

- Find where a parent could keep a small moment or question.
- What would you put here, if anything?
- What would you never want to put here?

Watch for:

- parent control;
- privacy sensitivity;
- pressure to track.

### Task 4: calendar concept

Prompt:

- Find the next family reminder.
- Does the calendar feel helpful, cluttered, or too much?

Watch for:

- organisation value;
- deadline/test feeling.

### Task 5: monthly check-in

Prompt:

- Preview the monthly check-in.
- How does the wording make you feel?
- Does it feel like reflection or assessment?

Watch for:

- judgement;
- skipped-question discoverability;
- signposting clarity.

### Task 6: support pack

Prompt:

- Open the Month 6 support pack.
- Which section feels most useful?
- Which section needs more evidence, reassurance, or explanation?

Watch for:

- Month 6 usefulness;
- starting-solids and movement clarity;
- safety uncertainty.

### Task 7: Aion concept

Prompt:

- Open the Aion example.
- What information do you think Aion used?
- Does it feel parent-controlled?
- Does anything feel creepy, clinical, or too automated?

Watch for:

- live AI confusion;
- surveillance concern;
- professional-boundary understanding.

### Task 8: summary concept

Prompt:

- Open the monthly summary.
- What is this summary for?
- What is it not for?

Watch for:

- clinical-record confusion;
- assessment confusion;
- value of a warm family record.

### Task 9: safety boundary

Prompt:

- Find where the prototype explains its limits.
- When would you contact a professional?

Watch for:

- boundary discoverability;
- false reassurance;
- unnecessary alarm.

## Closing questions

1. What felt most useful?
2. What felt warm or reassuring?
3. What felt worrying, judgmental, intrusive, or unclear?
4. What would you remove?
5. What would make you come back next month?
6. What would you expect a professional to review?
7. If this became real, what would you need to trust it?

## Success signals

- Parent can explain DevelopMonth as positive monthly support.
- Parent understands the prototype is static and fictional.
- Parent understands nothing is saved.
- Parent understands future personalisation would be optional.
- Parent does not interpret Aion as diagnosis, assessment, or monitoring.
- Parent identifies at least one repeat monthly value.

## Critical observations

Mark as critical if:

- parent thinks DevelopMonth diagnoses, assesses, scores, or monitors a child;
- parent thinks the prototype saved information;
- parent feels pressured to record everything;
- Aion feels like a live clinical decision-maker;
- safety wording is missed or misunderstood;
- mobile use blocks core tasks.
