# DevelopMonth Month 6 validation pack

Status: draft validation pack. No parent-facing child-development content is
approved for reliance.

This pack turns Month 6 into the first proof month for DevelopMonth. The goal
is to validate usefulness, trust, safety, professional-review process, and
repeat monthly value before building accounts, live Aion, storage, analytics,
payments, or any child-data infrastructure.

## Validation principle

Validate one excellent month before building the system.

Month 6 should demonstrate:

- positive child development first;
- practical support for starting solids and movement;
- parent-controlled journal, calendar, check-in, saved questions, and summary
  concepts;
- Aion as a future educational organiser, not a doctor, assessor, surveillance
  layer, or chatbot replacing human judgement;
- visible draft, source, jurisdiction, and professional-review status;
- clear boundaries around medical advice and professional guidance.

## What Month 6 must prove

### Parent value

Parents should understand that DevelopMonth gives calm monthly support for the
stage their child is in now. They should be able to identify at least one
reason they would return next month.

### Emotional safety

The experience should feel warm, positive, and supportive. Parents should not
feel tested, judged, compared, monitored, or pushed to record every moment.

### Parent control

Parents should understand that journal, calendar, check-in, saved questions,
and future personalisation are optional and based only on information they
choose to add.

### Professional boundary

Parents and reviewers should understand that DevelopMonth is educational
support only. It does not diagnose, treat, assess, monitor, guarantee
development, or replace professional care.

### Review credibility

Professionals should be able to review within scope, identify unsafe claims,
request source evidence, and sign off or reject only the parts they are
qualified to review.

### Ireland-first relevance

Parents should understand that Ireland is the first jurisdiction, recognise
the HSE as the source publisher, and understand GP, public health nurse, 112,
and 999 signposting without assuming that source mapping means professional
approval.

## Participant rounds

### Round 1: parent usability and trust

Recommended starting group: 6-8 parents or primary carers.

Use the fictional Rory profile. Do not request real child health information.
Observe mobile use for at least part of every session.

Primary questions:

- Can parents explain the product after seeing the Month 6 experience?
- Does the experience feel supportive rather than evaluative?
- Do parents understand that nothing is saved in the prototype?
- Does Aion feel parent-controlled and future-facing?
- Which part would they return to next month?
- Do they understand "around 6 months" as guidance rather than a deadline?
- Do they understand that HSE sources are assembled but the draft remains
  professionally unapproved?

### Round 2: scoped professional review

Recommended starting group: 5-7 scoped reviewers.

Reviewer scopes should cover, where possible:

- general child health and signposting;
- complementary feeding;
- choking prevention and food preparation;
- safer sleep;
- movement and positioning;
- communication, play, and shared attention;
- parent-facing plain language;
- privacy and child-data boundaries.
- Irish terminology, public-health routes, and emergency signposting.

One reviewer must not be treated as approving every domain.

## Stop conditions

Pause progression if:

- a parent believes the prototype diagnoses, scores, assesses, or monitors
  their child;
- Aion feels like a live clinical decision-maker;
- any content creates false reassurance or unnecessary alarm;
- parents feel pressured to track everything;
- a professional identifies unsafe feeding, sleep, movement, urgent-care, or
  signposting guidance;
- source, review, jurisdiction, or draft status is hidden or unclear;
- source mapping is mistaken for professional approval;
- "around 6 months" is interpreted as a deadline or pass/fail test;
- GP, public health nurse, 112, or 999 signposting is misunderstood;
- mobile users cannot complete the core tasks comfortably.

## Release gates before MVP planning

Do not begin infrastructure planning until:

- no unresolved critical safety or privacy issues remain;
- at least 80% of parent participants can explain the product and complete the
  core tasks;
- parent-control and no-persistence boundaries are understood;
- professional reviewers approve or revise claims within their scope;
- every material claim has a recorded source and review decision;
- Ireland is shown consistently as the first jurisdiction;
- parents understand HSE source transparency and Irish signposting;
- Month 6 version, source register, and approval record match;
- mobile and accessibility issues are retested;
- there is evidence of repeat monthly value.

## Evidence to collect

Use participant codes, not names.

Record:

- route or prototype area;
- task attempted;
- observation or short quote;
- issue category;
- severity;
- proposed change;
- owner;
- decision and rationale;
- retest status.

Do not store names, child names, child health details, contact details, or
identifiable recordings in ordinary product backlogs.

## Current decision

Month 6 remains a static validation prototype. It is suitable for research and
feedback using fictional content only. It is not suitable for parent reliance.
